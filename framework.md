# WOW Curriculum Framework

## Purpose

WOW helps children develop the inner and outer capacities needed to live thoughtfully in a complex world: self-awareness, curiosity, discernment, empathy, creativity, systems thinking, technological literacy, responsibility, and a sense of contribution.

The curriculum is not organized around memorizing subjects. It is organized around **ways of being, seeing, thinking, relating, making, and contributing**.

## Learning rhythm

Every experience can follow the same seven-part rhythm:

1. **NOTICE** — What do you observe in yourself, another person, nature, a system, or a tool?
2. **WONDER** — What are you curious about?
3. **EXPLORE** — Investigate through movement, conversation, observation, reading, play, experiment, or fieldwork.
4. **QUESTION** — What assumptions, claims, causes, or perspectives deserve examination?
5. **CREATE** — Make, build, draw, write, prototype, code, perform, or design something.
6. **REFLECT** — What changed in your understanding? What remains uncertain?
7. **CONTRIBUTE** — How could what you learned help a person, community, living system, or future problem?

## Developmental approach for ages 7–9

Koko and Israel can share the same experience while depth changes by child.

### Foundation path
- concrete observation
- sensory exploration
- sorting and comparing
- drawing and storytelling
- simple cause-and-effect
- naming feelings and needs
- explaining ideas in their own words

### Stretch path
- prediction before testing
- comparing evidence
- explaining causes
- finding exceptions
- identifying assumptions
- considering multiple perspectives
- designing a test or improvement

Neither path is a ranking. Children can move between them depending on the topic.

## Ten learning worlds

### 1. World of Me
**Question:** What is happening inside me?

Themes: body signals, emotions, senses, attention, identity, intuition, needs, values, rest, self-regulation.

Possible experiences:
- body signal map
- five-senses field walk
- attention experiment
- emotion weather report
- what helps me feel grounded?

### 2. Mind Lab
**Question:** How does my mind work?

Themes: memory, imagination, logic, curiosity, metacognition, assumptions, focus, learning strategies.

Possible experiences:
- memory illusion games
- invent three explanations for one mystery
- prediction vs result
- “how do I know?” ladder
- imagination prototype

### 3. Living Earth
**Question:** How is life connected?

Themes: ecology, plants, animals, cycles, food systems, weather, interdependence, biomimicry, stewardship.

Possible experiences:
- neighborhood ecosystem map
- trace one meal back to the Earth
- observe one tree for a month
- design something inspired by nature
- follow a water cycle locally

### 4. World of Us
**Question:** How do we live well with other people?

Themes: empathy, communication, boundaries, consent, cooperation, conflict, belonging, culture, perspective.

Possible experiences:
- perspective-switch storytelling
- boundary language practice
- cooperative design challenge
- listening experiment
- repair after conflict

### 5. Creation Station
**Question:** What can I bring into the world?

Themes: art, storytelling, design, invention, entrepreneurship, prototyping, iteration, play.

Possible experiences:
- solve an annoying everyday problem
- invent a product for an animal
- create a tiny business
- redesign a household object
- make a story in three media

### 6. Truth Quest
**Question:** How do I decide what to trust?

Themes: evidence, sources, fact/opinion, persuasion, advertising, misinformation, incentives, probability.

Possible experiences:
- fact/opinion/prediction/invention game
- compare two sources
- design an advertisement then analyze it
- spot emotional persuasion
- investigate a viral claim with an adult

### 7. Tech World
**Question:** How do human-made systems work?

Themes: computers, internet, coding, engineering, privacy, cybersecurity, algorithms, digital citizenship.

Possible experiences:
- “be the internet” packet-routing game
- Scratch project
- password strength experiment using fictional examples
- map what happens after clicking a link
- design a simple machine

### 8. AI Adventure
**Question:** How can I understand and create with artificial intelligence without giving away my own thinking?

AI Adventure remains a separate curriculum and website. WOW links into it as a full learning pathway.

Core principle: **ME → WE → AI**.

### 9. The Unknown
**Question:** What do we do with questions that do not have easy answers?

Themes: philosophy, consciousness, space, time, mortality, mystery, belief, uncertainty, awe.

Possible experiences:
- “what makes something alive?”
- night-sky observation
- impossible questions jar
- thought experiments
- distinguish “we do not know yet” from “nobody can know”

### 10. Make It Matter
**Question:** What will I do with what I know?

Themes: purpose, contribution, ethics, service, responsibility, community problems, future-building.

Possible experiences:
- choose one small problem nearby
- interview someone affected
- design and test a response
- reflect on unintended consequences
- share what changed

## Parent / guide role

The adult should:
- ask before explaining
- invite predictions before revealing answers
- make uncertainty normal
- distinguish safety boundaries from intellectual control
- help children verify claims
- model changing their mind when evidence changes
- protect privacy
- prioritize conversation and creation over screen time

A useful phrase throughout WOW is:

> **“I don’t know. How could we find out?”**

## Assessment philosophy

No conventional grades are required.

Evidence of learning can include:
- questions becoming more precise
- explanations becoming richer
- ability to transfer an idea to a new context
- creations and prototypes
- reflections
- experiments and revisions
- ability to identify uncertainty
- acts of contribution

A child’s portfolio can contain **Wonders, Discoveries, Creations, Questions, Experiments, and Contributions** rather than scores.

## Initial cadence

For ages 7–9, start with one WOW session per week, roughly 45–75 minutes, plus optional small observations or experiments during ordinary life.

Do not attempt all ten worlds sequentially. Rotate them so the children experience the interconnectedness of the curriculum.
