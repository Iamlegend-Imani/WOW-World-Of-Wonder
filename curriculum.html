WOW Foundation · Ages 7–9

Twenty-four weeks of learning across the whole world.

Six learning arcs. Four weeks each. No subject silos. Every week moves through several worlds and ends with something noticed, questioned, made, tested, or contributed.

24 weeks6 cross-world arcs10 worlds45–75 min / session
Child first

The child’s body, mind, relationships, imagination, and agency come before any tool or subject.

Questions before answers

Begin by noticing and wondering. Information arrives after curiosity has somewhere to land.

Connected learning

Nature can teach design. Technology can teach ethics. Feelings can teach attention. Reality does not separate itself into school subjects.

Creation over completion

Evidence of learning is a richer question, explanation, experiment, revision, creation, or contribution — not a percentage.

Arc 01 · Weeks 1–4

Learning to Notice

Self-awareness, senses, attention, emotion, perspective, and the living world.

World of Me · Mind Lab · Living Earth · World of Us

Big idea: Attention changes what becomes available to us.

Experience

Do a slow sensory walk. Collect five visual details, four sounds, three textures, two smells, and one question. Repeat for two minutes with eyes closed where safe and compare what changed.

Foundation path

Draw a “map of noticing” using symbols, colors, and words.

Stretch path

Sort observations into fact, interpretation, and question. Notice how quickly the mind adds a story to a sensation.

Parent lens

Ask “What did you notice?” before “What is it?”

Reflection

What became visible only because you slowed down?

Big idea: Feelings are information, not commands.

Experience

Create a body-weather map: where do excitement, worry, anger, calm, embarrassment, and curiosity show up physically? Compare how the same feeling can feel different for different people.

Foundation path

Name sensations and pair them with possible emotions without insisting on one correct label.

Stretch path

Separate signal from action: “I feel angry” is different from “I must hit/shout/leave.” Generate three possible responses to one emotion.

Reflection

Can your body know something before you have words for it?

Big idea: Perspective shapes experience, but perspective and factual accuracy are not the same thing.

Experience

Everyone observes the same image, short event, or object separately and writes/draws what happened. Compare without correcting first.

Foundation path

Find three differences in what people noticed.

Stretch path

Sort statements into observation, inference, opinion, and claim that needs evidence.

Reflection

When can two people both be right? When do we need evidence?

Big idea: Patterns help us describe, predict, design, and ask better questions.

Experience

Collect patterns from leaves, music, movement, architecture, family routines, weather, or numbers. Recreate one pattern in another medium.

Foundation path

Continue and invent simple repeating patterns.

Stretch path

Find a pattern that looks real but breaks when you collect more examples.

Create

Make a “Pattern Museum” with at least four different kinds of pattern.

Arc 02 · Weeks 5–8

Living With Others

Empathy, boundaries, communication, consent, conflict, cooperation, and culture.

World of Us · World of Me · Truth Quest · Make It Matter

Big idea: Empathy is curiosity about another person’s experience, not mind-reading.

Experience

Use short scenarios and generate at least three possible explanations for a person’s behavior. Ask what information is missing.

Foundation path

Practice “Maybe they…” language instead of declaring motives as facts.

Stretch path

Compare empathy, agreement, and approval. Can you understand someone and still disagree?

Reflection

What is dangerous about assuming we know why someone did something?

Big idea: Boundaries help people know where choice and responsibility live.

Experience

Sort examples into MY CHOICE, SHARED CHOICE, and NOT MY CHOICE. Include body, belongings, secrets, shared rooms, games, photos, and digital information.

Foundation path

Practice clear yes/no/not-yet language.

Stretch path

Explore conflicts between personal choice and shared responsibility.

Create

Write a family “Ask First” agreement.

Big idea: Communication is a feedback loop, not just sending words.

Experience

Play “instruction challenge”: one person describes a hidden block structure or drawing while another recreates it without seeing it.

Investigate

What kinds of instructions caused confusion? What questions improved the result?

Stretch path

Try again with rules limiting questions, then compare accuracy.

Reflection

Is misunderstanding always somebody’s fault?

Big idea: Repair is more than saying sorry; it includes understanding impact and changing what happens next.

Experience

Use fictional conflicts. Identify what happened, impact, responsibility, what is needed now, and what could prevent repetition.

Create

Make a five-step “Repair Map”: Notice → Own → Listen → Repair → Change.

Reflection

What makes an apology trustworthy?

Arc 03 · Weeks 9–12

Learning From a Living World

Ecology, systems, interdependence, biomimicry, resources, stewardship, and place.

Living Earth · Mind Lab · Creation Station · Make It Matter

Big idea: Systems are made of relationships, not just parts.

Experience

Choose a familiar food and map backwards: plate → store → transport → farm → soil/water/sun/pollinators/people. Add what each part depends on.

Foundation path

Build a simple connection web.

Stretch path

Remove one node and predict ripple effects.

Reflection

Can something be “independent” and still depend on a system?

Big idea: Nature offers strategies for structure, movement, protection, cooling, storage, and cooperation.

Experience

Study three natural designs: seed dispersal, burr hooks, shells, honeycomb, bird wings, spider webs, lotus leaves, etc.

Create

Design a human tool inspired by a natural strategy, not merely its appearance.

Stretch path

Explain the function being borrowed and where the analogy breaks.

Big idea: Materials move through systems; throwing something “away” does not erase it.

Experience

Choose five household objects and investigate what they are made from, where those materials may come from, and where they go after use.

Foundation path

Sort into reuse, repair, recycle, compost, landfill/unknown.

Stretch path

Ask which environmental claims need verification and which recycling symbols are easily misunderstood.

Create

Redesign one object for a longer life.

Big idea: Stewardship begins with relationship and observation.

Experience

Choose one tiny place — yard, sidewalk, park corner, windowsill, block — and observe it over several days.

Contribute

Do one useful action based on what you observed: clean, plant, label, protect, document, reduce waste, or help another person notice.

Reflection

How is caring for a place different from controlling it?

Arc 04 · Weeks 13–16

Truth, Attention & Influence

Evidence, persuasion, advertising, incentives, media literacy, uncertainty, and trust.

Truth Quest · Mind Lab · World of Me · Tech World

Big idea: Different claims need different kinds of support.

Experience

Sort statements into observation, memory, opinion, prediction, inference, and externally checkable claim.

Foundation path

Ask “How could we check?”

Stretch path

Rank evidence quality for a simple claim: rumor, personal experience, photo, measurement, multiple independent sources.

Reflection

Does being confident make something more true?

Big idea: Attention has value, and many systems compete for it.

Experience

Collect five examples designed to make someone look, click, buy, watch, or continue. Identify color, sound, urgency, social proof, rewards, or emotional triggers.

Stretch path

Ask who benefits and what behavior is being optimized.

Create

Design an invitation that earns attention without misleading anyone.

Big idea: Persuasion is not automatically bad; the methods and intentions matter.

Experience

Compare a public-service message, advertisement, campaign poster, product package, and request from a friend. Identify audience, goal, evidence, emotion, and missing information.

Foundation path

Find what the message wants you to do.

Stretch path

Distinguish persuasion, manipulation, deception, and coercion.

Reflection

Can a true statement still be misleading?

Big idea: Uncertainty is something we can describe and work with.

Experience

Practice confidence language: I know / I think / I suspect / I am unsure / I do not know yet. Apply it to everyday questions.

Stretch path

Update confidence after receiving new evidence and explain why it changed.

Create

Make a family “It’s Okay Not to Know Yet” card with ways to investigate safely.

Arc 05 · Weeks 17–20

Humans, Tools & Intelligent Machines

Engineering, computation, privacy, cybersecurity, algorithms, AI, agency, and human judgment.

Tech World · AI Adventure · Truth Quest · Creation Station · World of Me

Big idea: Technology is a human-made way of extending capability, not just electronics.

Experience

Sort objects from spoon to bicycle to pencil to smartphone. For each, identify the human problem or desire it addresses.

Stretch path

Map tradeoffs: what capability does the tool add, and what new dependency or risk does it create?

Create

Invent a non-digital technology for a small household problem.

Big idea: Privacy is about context, choice, boundaries, and control over information.

Experience

Sort information into public, family-only, trusted-person, private, and “ask first.” Include name, location, school, photos, passwords, hobbies, feelings, and health information.

Cyber habit

Create strong passphrases with invented examples; never use real passwords in the activity.

Stretch path

Discuss why harmless facts can become sensitive when combined.

Create

Make a family digital privacy agreement.

Big idea: Machine-learning systems learn statistical patterns from examples; they can be useful and still make mistakes.

Experience

Use the separate AI Adventure sequence for Scratch, Teachable Machine, testing, and model mistakes.

WOW connection

Before opening the tool, predict what examples the machine will need. Afterward, deliberately try to break the model.

Reflection

Did the machine understand, or did it become good at recognizing examples?

Big idea: Capability does not automatically create authority.

Experience

Sort decisions into MACHINE CAN HELP, HUMAN SHOULD DECIDE, and BOTH. Include navigation, music suggestions, medical information, punishment, friendship, hiring, translation, creative ideas, and emergency choices.

Stretch path

Ask what values, context, accountability, or lived experience could be missing.

Family rule

Use ME → WE → AI: think first, discuss together, then invite AI to contribute.

Arc 06 · Weeks 21–24

Imagination, Purpose & Contribution

Creativity, entrepreneurship, mystery, values, problem-solving, service, and meaningful creation.

Creation Station · The Unknown · Make It Matter · ALL WORLDS

Big idea: Creativity often combines, transforms, notices, and experiments rather than producing ideas from nowhere.

Experience

Pick two unrelated objects or ideas and invent five combinations. Then improve one by asking “What if…?” ten times.

Stretch path

Trace influences behind an idea without treating influence as failure.

Create

Make one deliberately strange prototype.

Big idea: Some questions are factual, some philosophical, some personal, and some remain open.

Experience

Build a Question Constellation. Add questions about consciousness, space, fairness, death, dreams, time, identity, infinity, or anything genuinely puzzling.

Investigate

Classify each question: searchable fact, testable investigation, philosophical question, personal reflection, or currently unknown.

Reflection

Can not knowing be valuable?

Big idea: Good creation begins with listening to a real need.

Experience

Interview someone about one tiny frustration, need, or wish. Ask follow-up questions before suggesting solutions.

Create

Build a paper/cardboard/code/story prototype. Test it with the person. Record what changed after feedback.

Stretch path

Identify user, need, constraints, tradeoffs, and what success would look like.

Big idea: Learning becomes meaningful when it changes how we see, choose, create, or contribute.

Capstone

Each child chooses a person, animal, place, problem, idea, or question they care about and creates a response that helps, teaches, protects, explains, delights, improves, or invites wonder.

Presentation

Tell the story: “I noticed… I wondered… I investigated… I changed my mind about… I created… I tested… I learned… Now I want to…”

No grade

Celebrate the quality of attention, questions, revisions, connections, and contribution.

After Week 24

The curriculum loops. It does not end.

Repeat an arc at greater depth. Follow a child’s question into a new project. Add history, science, mathematics, writing, art, coding, culture, and place as the questions demand them. WOW is a way of learning across life, not a list to finish.
